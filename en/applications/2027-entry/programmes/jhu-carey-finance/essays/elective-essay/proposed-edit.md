## Essay text

In my final high school basketball season, I was the captain, starting centre and one of the team's main scorers. When our coach became unavailable shortly before the league began, I also took responsibility for rotations and tactics. I wanted to finish the season well, but our opening match ended in a three-point defeat.

After the game, teammates began blaming one another. I was exhausted and disappointed too, and I realised that continuing the argument would make it harder to prepare for the matches ahead. I asked the team to refocus on our shared goal and reconsidered the way we were playing.

I had expected to help the team by scoring. After the opening loss, I began to see that our offence depended too heavily on interior play and made too little use of other players' speed and outside shooting. I needed to reconsider how I could contribute.

I decided to step out of the starting lineup for the next match. Giving up that place was difficult in my final season, especially as a player who had hoped for more time on court. From the sideline, I could watch the opponents' defence, adjust our tactics and think about roles across the team. I wanted to give our other players more room to contribute while taking responsibility for organising the team.

We won our following two matches. Our offensive organisation and defensive discipline improved, and I saw players pass more readily and trust one another's decisions. We were eventually eliminated in the knockout stage, so the season still ended short of the result we had wanted. The way we responded to the first defeat changed how I understood my responsibility as captain.

The loss made me confront how closely I associated leadership with being a reliable starter and scorer. I learned to judge my contribution by what the team needed at that moment. In this case, that meant accepting a place outside the starting lineup and giving more attention to how others could play effectively together. I want to bring that willingness to reconsider my role to future teams, listen to different perspectives and adjust my contribution when our shared work requires it.

## Editorial notes

| Original passage | Change | Purpose |

| Opening | State the captaincy, playing role and opening defeat. Shorten the claim about a final chance to prove himself | Establish the setback and responsibilities quickly |

| Lineup decision | Keep the choice to step out of the starting five and observe from the sideline | Connect a personal choice with rotations, tactics and teammates' roles |
| Outcome | State the two subsequent wins and knockout exit separately from observed improvements | Keep the results and interpretation proportionate |

## Applicant checks

The three-point loss, two subsequent wins, decision to leave the starting lineup and responsibility for rotations and tactics come from the original draft. Confirm the exact scope of the coaching responsibility and whether the lineup change affected minutes played.

The accounts of outside shooting, speed, passing and defensive discipline are also draft-sourced. A specific possession recalled by the applicant could replace one general statement. Confirm the personal interpretation and closing language before adopting the revision.
